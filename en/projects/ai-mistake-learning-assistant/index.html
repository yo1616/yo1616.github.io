01

The problem

Mistakes, paper records, and student–teacher conversations were spread across different places, making recurring knowledge gaps difficult to track. We began with a tutoring center’s workflow and developed a prototype connecting questions, mistake records, practice, and teaching analytics.

02

My contribution

I led requirements interviews, organized functional and data needs, and coordinated task allocation as project lead. My work covered system architecture, data modeling, REST API specifications, and integration flows across the student interface, teacher interface, and Gemini API module.

03

Methods & decisions

Interviews examined how teachers answered questions, recorded mistakes, and identified learning gaps. Feedback on the early prototype informed image-and-text input, knowledge-point classification, practice generation, and date filtering. The backend stores conversations and mistake records; AI-assisted analysis and classification then support practice and teacher dashboards. API specifications and consistent data formats provided a shared basis for integration.

04

Outcomes

The team delivered a demonstrable prototype with AI-assisted Q&A, mistake and concept records, follow-up practice, and student and teacher analytics. The stack includes React, Express, SQLite, and the Gemini API. Scores and counts shown in the screens illustrate the interface; they are not evidence of adoption or learning impact.

05

Limitations & next steps

The evidence supports functional and integration outcomes. Deployment scale, time savings, and learning gains have not been established. Further evaluation could examine classification and generation quality, teachers’ interpretation of the analytics, and access and privacy controls for real data.
