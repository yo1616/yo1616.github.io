01

Project Background

In traditional tutoring-center workflows, student questions, mistake records, and learning progress are often scattered across handwritten notes, worksheets, and teacher-student conversations. While teachers can observe students' immediate performance, consistently tracking recurring knowledge gaps and identifying common weaknesses across an entire class can be challenging.

To better understand these challenges, we conducted interviews with tutoring-center staff and analyzed existing teaching workflows. Based on the findings, we planned a system combining AI-assisted Q&A, mistake tracking, follow-up practice, and learning analytics. The goal was to help students review recurring weaknesses while giving teachers a more structured view of learning progress.

02

My Contributions

I served as the project lead of a five-member team, coordinating requirements analysis, system planning, and development activities. During the initial phase, I led requirements interviews with tutoring-center staff, examined their teaching workflows, and translated the findings into functional requirements and system specifications to guide development.

On the technical side, I primarily focused on system architecture and data model planning, REST API specifications, and cross-module data flows. This involved defining how the student interface, teacher dashboard, backend database, and Gemini API would interact, helping the team establish consistent data structures and reduce integration issues.

Beyond technical planning, I was responsible for task allocation, development coordination, and integration discussions. By aligning team members around shared requirements and interface specifications, I helped coordinate the development of a functional system prototype.

03

Design & Implementation

Our design process began with real teaching scenarios. Through interviews, we examined how teachers responded to student questions, documented mistakes, and identified learning gaps. We then translated these needs into features for both student and teacher interfaces, refining the interaction flows based on feedback from early prototype testing.

The student interface combines text and image-based questions, AI-assisted explanations, mistake records, and follow-up practice, allowing students to revisit previous questions and review frequently missed concepts. The teacher dashboard presents class-level and individual learning statistics, including mistake distributions and knowledge-point summaries, to support more structured teaching analysis.

The technical stack includes React, Node.js/Express, SQLite, and the Gemini API. Backend APIs handle student questions, AI responses, and mistake records, while the stored information supports student analytics and teacher-facing dashboards. My focus was on API consistency, data structure planning, and frontend-backend integration, helping different components exchange information through shared specifications.

04

Project Outcomes

The team delivered a functional prototype combining AI-assisted learning and mistake analysis. Its features include student Q&A, AI conversation history, mistake and knowledge-point classification, follow-up practice, and analytics interfaces for both students and teachers.

This project gave me hands-on experience across the development lifecycle, from user interviews and system specification design to cross-module integration. It also strengthened my ability to coordinate a technical team, align development responsibilities, and translate educational needs into implementable system features.

The current outcome is a functional prototype developed for demonstration purposes. Student counts, performance metrics, and mistake statistics shown in the interface are illustrative data rather than evidence of real-world adoption or measured learning outcomes.
