Challenge Scoring System — AI-Assisted Rapid Prototyping

Collaborated with teammates I met on the day of a hackathon-style AI workshop to identify a practical event-scoring problem and explore a solution within a limited timeframe. Through rapid problem framing, feature planning, and AI-assisted prototyping, we worked on a challenge scoring system designed to centralize scores, standardize scoring rules, and simplify team rankings.

01

Project Background

This project was developed during a one-day, hackathon-style AI workshop. Participants formed teams on the day and were challenged to identify a problem, define a potential solution, and explore how AI tools could support the development process within a short period.

Our team focused on scoring and point tracking in challenge-based events, such as university camps and team-building activities. These events often involve multiple stations, separate scoring decisions, and manual consolidation of results.

We identified several practical issues: inconsistent scoring standards across stations, errors in manual score aggregation, and time-consuming ranking calculations. Based on these observations, we defined the central challenge as creating a more consistent and manageable scoring workflow.

02

My Contributions

One of the defining aspects of this experience was working with teammates I had just met. With only one day available, we needed to exchange ideas quickly, establish a shared understanding of the problem, and agree on a practical direction.

I participated in problem identification, requirements discussion, and feature planning, helping the team translate common event-scoring difficulties into a clearer system concept. We focused on shared scoring rules, centralized point tracking, and different information needs across user roles.

We also worked with AI tools to support rapid prototype development, exploring how to move from a problem statement to an initial system concept and implementation within the workshop's time constraints.

This experience strengthened my ability to frame problems quickly, collaborate with unfamiliar teammates, and use AI as part of an iterative development process, particularly when time and resources are limited.

03

System Concept

The proposed challenge scoring system focused on consistent scoring, centralized recordkeeping, and accessible ranking information. Its goal was to reduce the need for manual score consolidation and make activity progress easier to track.

We organized the concept around three user roles: administrators, station hosts, and participants, each with different responsibilities and information needs.

Administrators would oversee total scores and rankings, configure scoring limits for individual stations, and publish announcements. Station hosts would enter points according to the event rules, while participants could monitor their team's scores and ranking information.

The intended use cases included school camps and corporate team-building activities, where multiple teams and scoring stations require coordinated administration.

04

Project Outcomes

Within the one-day workshop, our team produced a defined problem statement, user-role specifications, and a feature proposal for a challenge scoring system, while using AI tools to explore its initial implementation.

For me, the most valuable outcome was learning how to turn an initially broad problem into a focused, actionable concept under time pressure, while coordinating with a newly formed team and incorporating AI into the development workflow.

The project was a short-duration prototyping exercise rather than a production deployment. Its documented deliverables focus on the problem, system design, and proposed functionality.
