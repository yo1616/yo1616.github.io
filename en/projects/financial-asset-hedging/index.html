01

Research Background

In portfolio management, the value of a hedging asset depends not only on its correlation with equities under normal market conditions, but also on whether it can provide diversification benefits during periods of severe market stress. Assets with relatively low correlations during ordinary periods may behave differently during financial crises.

To explore this question, our team used the S&P 500 as the equity-market benchmark and examined four assets: gold, a U.S. Treasury ETF (IEF), the Swiss franc, and Bitcoin. We analyzed their historical return relationships with equities and investigated whether these relationships changed during the COVID period.

The central research questions were: Which assets exhibit low or negative return relationships with equities, and how do these relationships change under crisis conditions?

02

My Contributions

My primary responsibility was collecting and processing financial market data using Python. This included retrieving historical prices, organizing time-series datasets, and calculating asset returns for subsequent statistical analysis.

I contributed to the Python implementation of correlation analysis, OLS regression, and COVID-period interaction models, translating research questions into executable analytical procedures and organizing model outputs for the team's interpretation.

I was also responsible for data visualization and chart development, creating scatter plots, regression trend lines, and comparisons of statistical results to illustrate how asset-return relationships differed between COVID and non-COVID periods.

Through this work, I gained practical experience across the analytical workflow, from financial data retrieval and preprocessing to statistical modeling and results presentation.

03

Analytical Approach

We first collected historical price data for the selected assets, aligned observations by trading date, and calculated daily returns. We then used correlation coefficients and scatter plots to examine linear relationships between each asset and the S&P 500.

Next, we estimated Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression models to quantify the relationships between equity-market returns and individual asset returns. We examined regression coefficients, statistical significance, and explanatory power to understand the strength and direction of these relationships.

To investigate changes during periods of market stress, we introduced a COVID-period indicator and an interaction term with market returns. This allowed us to test whether the level and slope of the return relationships differed during the crisis period.

The analysis used Python, pandas, statsmodels, and financial market data retrieval tools, supported by visualizations of model estimates and return patterns. Interpretation considered coefficients, p-values, and R² rather than relying solely on the direction of regression lines.

04

Research Findings

Under the sample and model specifications used in the study, the U.S. Treasury ETF exhibited the clearest negative correlation among the four assets, while Bitcoin showed a positive relationship with equity-market returns. In this sample, Bitcoin did not consistently behave as an asset moving in the opposite direction from equities.

The interaction-model results indicated a statistically significant change in gold's sensitivity to market returns during the COVID period. Meanwhile, the Swiss franc exhibited a near-zero overall linear correlation with the equity market. These observations suggest that hedging characteristics can vary across market environments and should not be assessed using a single correlation estimate alone.

This project strengthened my ability to apply statistical modeling and data analysis to financial research questions, interpret empirical results, and connect quantitative findings with their underlying assumptions and market context.

These findings represent historical observations under specific sample and model assumptions. They do not constitute a backtested trading strategy or guarantee similar hedging performance in future market conditions.
