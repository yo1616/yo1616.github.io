Financial Asset Hedging Analysis

Compared gold, a U.S. Treasury ETF, the Swiss franc, and Bitcoin against the S&P 500 using correlation analysis and crisis-period interaction models.

01

The problem

Low correlation in ordinary periods may not persist during a crisis. We examined historical return relationships across four assets and tested whether their relationships with the equity market changed during COVID.

02

My contribution

I contributed to market-data collection and cleaning, return calculations, statistical implementation, and visualization. The research questions and conclusions were developed collaboratively; this case focuses on my work turning data into an inspectable analytical workflow.

03

Method

The workflow aligns trading dates, calculates daily returns, and examines correlations and scatter plots before fitting OLS models. A second specification adds a COVID indicator and a market-return interaction, separating level shifts from slope changes. Interpretation considers coefficients, p-values, and R² together.

04

Findings

Under the course report’s sample definition, the Treasury ETF showed the strongest negative correlation among the four assets, while Bitcoin was positively correlated with equities. Gold’s COVID interaction term was statistically significant, and the Swiss franc’s linear correlation with the market was close to zero. The findings need to be interpreted alongside the period and model specification.

The report uses 2014–2024 data and defines 2020 as the COVID period. Repository sample windows and code versions differ, and matching data have not been verified. The study does not validate a trading strategy, establish causality, or guarantee future hedging performance.

The original chart label US10Y refers to the project’s 7–10 year Treasury ETF (IEF), not a Treasury yield.
