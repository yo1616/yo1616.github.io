Problem & Research Approach

Traditional RFM analysis evaluates customer value through Recency, Frequency, and Monetary value. However, relying solely on these three dimensions may overlook canceled, returned, or incomplete transactions, potentially creating a gap between a customer's assigned value and their actual contribution.

To address this issue, our team explored an enhanced RFM framework incorporating transaction completion indicators. By considering successful-order information alongside purchasing behavior, we examined alternative ways to assess customer value and compared the resulting segmentation with existing membership tiers.

The research also investigated how these insights could help distinguish high-value, high-potential, and at-risk customers, creating a foundation for marketing strategies tailored to different purchasing patterns and customer needs.

My Contributions

I contributed to discussions and analysis of customer value and segmentation results, helping organize the team's observations and connect the analytical findings with practical business questions and potential strategic implications.

I also took responsibility for report synthesis and content organization, bringing together the research motivation, methodology, findings, and marketing recommendations into a coherent written deliverable.

03

Research Outcomes

The team completed a customer value assessment study centered on RFM analysis and transaction completion indicators. The research examined how successful and unsuccessful transactions could influence customer classification and discussed the potential implications for customer identification and marketing resource allocation.

Building on the segmentation framework, we developed differentiated marketing recommendations for different customer groups. These included loyalty and retention initiatives for high-value customers, repurchase incentives for high-potential customers, and service experience improvements for at-risk customers.

This project strengthened my experience in customer behavior analysis, business problem framing, research synthesis, and team coordination. It also helped me better understand how analytical findings can be translated into actionable business recommendations.
