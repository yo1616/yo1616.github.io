01

Role & Responsibilities

As Finance Director of the NTU Information Management Student Association, I was responsible for budget planning, event-related expense management, and resource coordination, supporting the student association's activities within available financial resources.

Since different departments had different event requirements and spending priorities, I worked to understand their funding needs and implementation plans, helping connect event objectives with available budgets.

In addition to planning, I participated in expense management and budget coordination during event preparation, contributing financial input to practical organizational decisions rather than treating financial work solely as recordkeeping.

02

Cross-Department Collaboration

The finance function required regular communication with event and administrative teams. I worked with different departments to understand their operational needs, coordinate funding arrangements, and align resources with event timelines.

With limited budgets, different teams sometimes had competing priorities. I learned to consider requests from an organization-wide perspective and support discussions about feasible resource arrangements, balancing event objectives with financial constraints.

This experience demonstrated that financial management is also a communication and coordination responsibility. Beyond understanding budgets and expenses, it involves communicating constraints clearly so that teams can make informed planning decisions.

03

Experience & Skills Developed

Serving as Finance Director strengthened my practical experience in budget planning, expense management, resource allocation, and cross-department collaboration, while giving me a broader understanding of how student organizations operate under resource constraints.

Unlike participating in an event solely as a team member, this role required me to consider resource management and organizational priorities, assess different teams' needs, and communicate across departments.

The experience reinforced a principle that also applies to professional projects: beyond proposing an effective plan, making decisions within financial, operational, and resource constraints is an essential part of management.
